A musty smell coming from your washing machine usually means detergent residue, fabric softener buildup, or trapped moisture has built up somewhere the water doesn't fully rinse — most often in the rubber door seal or the detergent drawer.

## Why It Happens

Front-load washers are especially prone to this because the door seal stays damp between cycles. Combined with low-temperature washes (which don't kill bacteria as effectively), that dampness turns into a breeding ground for mildew.

## What You'll Need

- White vinegar
- Baking soda
- An old toothbrush or small brush
- A microfiber cloth

## Steps

1. **Wipe down the rubber door seal.** Pull it back gently and wipe out any trapped water, lint, or debris with a cloth dipped in vinegar.
2. **Run an empty hot cycle with vinegar.** Add two cups of white vinegar directly to the drum and run the hottest, longest cycle your machine has.
3. **Follow with a baking soda cycle.** Add half a cup of baking soda to the drum and run another hot cycle — this helps neutralize any remaining odor.
4. **Clean the detergent drawer.** Remove it if possible and scrub with warm soapy water, paying attention to the corners where residue collects.
5. **Leave the door open between washes.** This is the single biggest habit change that prevents the smell from coming back — it lets the drum and seal fully dry out.

## Keeping It From Coming Back

Run this vinegar-and-baking-soda cleaning routine once a month, use the recommended amount of detergent (more isn't better — excess detergent is a common cause of buildup), and always leave the door and detergent drawer open a crack after each wash.
