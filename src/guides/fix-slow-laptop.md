Before assuming your laptop needs replacing, it's worth ruling out a handful of common causes — most slowdowns come from software, not hardware failure.

## Check What's Actually Using Resources

Open your Task Manager (Windows: Ctrl+Shift+Esc) or Activity Monitor (Mac: Cmd+Space, then search) and sort by CPU and memory usage. This tells you immediately if one runaway program is the culprit, rather than the whole system.

## Steps to Try, in Order

1. **Restart it properly.** Sounds obvious, but a laptop left in sleep mode for days accumulates memory clutter that a full restart clears.
2. **Disable unnecessary startup programs.** Many apps quietly launch themselves at boot. Trim this list to just what you actually need running immediately.
3. **Check available storage.** If your drive is more than 90% full, performance drops noticeably — especially on machines with smaller SSDs. Clear out large files you no longer need.
4. **Run a malware scan.** Unwanted background processes from adware or malware are a common, overlooked cause of slowdown.
5. **Update your operating system and drivers.** Outdated drivers, particularly graphics drivers, can cause the whole system to feel sluggish.
6. **Reduce visual effects.** Turning off animations and transparency effects frees up a surprising amount of resources on older machines.

## When It Might Be Hardware

If you've tried the above and things are still slow, and your laptop is more than 4-5 years old with a traditional hard drive (not an SSD), upgrading to an SSD is usually the single biggest speed improvement available — often more noticeable than adding RAM.

## What Not to Do

Avoid running multiple "cleaner" or "optimizer" programs at once — many are unnecessary at best, and some install extra background processes that make things worse.
