Hard water stains — the cloudy white or chalky buildup on faucets, glass, and tile — are mineral deposits left behind as water evaporates. They're stubborn, but they respond well to acidic cleaners, which dissolve the minerals.

## What You'll Need

- White vinegar (or lemon juice)
- Baking soda
- A soft cloth or non-scratch sponge
- An old toothbrush for grout and corners

## For Faucets and Fixtures

1. Soak a cloth or paper towel in white vinegar and wrap it directly around the affected area.
2. Leave it for 15-30 minutes — longer for heavier buildup.
3. Remove the cloth and scrub gently with a soft sponge.
4. Rinse thoroughly and dry with a clean cloth to prevent new spots from forming.

## For Glass Shower Doors

1. Spray or wipe undiluted white vinegar directly onto the glass.
2. Let it sit for 10-15 minutes.
3. Scrub with a non-scratch sponge, focusing on the cloudiest areas.
4. Rinse well and squeegee dry — this last step matters, since air-drying is what causes new spots.

## For Tile and Grout

Make a paste of baking soda and a small amount of water, apply it to the grout lines, then spray vinegar over it. The fizzing reaction helps lift stains out of the porous grout. Scrub with an old toothbrush, then rinse.

## Preventing Stains From Coming Back

The real fix is drying surfaces after use — a squeegee on shower glass and a quick wipe of faucets takes seconds and prevents most buildup from forming in the first place. If your home has particularly hard water, a water softener addresses the root cause rather than the symptom.
